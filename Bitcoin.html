What is Bitcoin

Bitcoin is the first decentralized cryptocurrency. Based on a free-market ideology, bitcoin was invented in 2008 when an unknown person published a white paper under the pseudonym of Satoshi Nakamoto.[4] Use of bitcoin as a currency began in 2009,[5] with the release of its open-source implementation.[6]: ch. 1  From 2021 to 2025, El Salvador adopted it as legal tender currency before revoking it.[7][8][9] As bitcoin is pseudonymous, its use by criminals has attracted the attention of regulators, leading to its ban by several countries.[10]

How did Bitcoin gain popularity?

A decentralised currency

Bitcoin gained popularity as the first decentralized cryptocurrency, offering a peer-to-peer electronic cash system that allows online payments to be sent directly from one party to another without going through a financial institution.

Bitcoin as an investment

Many see Bitcoin investment as digital gold. The value of cryptocurrency has risen substantially since its introduction. In 2010, Bitcoin maintained a value of below one dollar. Bitcoin reached its peak value of $68,000 when the market set a new record in 2021. The market sees Bitcoin as an effective store of value. Unlike the unlimited issuance of paper money, the total supply of Bitcoin as a digital currency is restricted. The total number of Bitcoins will remain capped at exactly 21 million. This scarcity increases demand. Many market analysts believe the price of Bitcoin will continue to climb upward.

Global adoption and acceptance

More businesses now accept Bitcoin. Big companies, including Tesla Mic, Microsoft, and PayPal, enable their customers to complete Bitcoin transactions. Various national governments have declared Bitcoin an official legal currency. In 2021, El Salvador established itself as the world's first sovereign state to make Bitcoin its official digital currency. Bitcoin functions as an international payment medium for various users. The system performs operations quickly at a reduced cost compared to traditional banking infrastructure. A World Bank analysis shows that 6% is the standard rate for worldwide remittance processing fees. Transactions through Bitcoin typically come with reduced costs.
